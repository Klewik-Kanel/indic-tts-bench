# Indic TTS benchmark — listening comparison

Devanagari does not write schwa deletion. *नमक* is spoken *namak*, not
*namaka*. One arm of this comparison is handed the deletion by a rule-based
front end; the other reads characters and has to infer it. Architecture, seed,
corpus and step budget are identical, so the difference is the input
representation.

Every sentence also carries the natural recording, which turns the question
from "which arm do you prefer" into "how far is each from the speaker". That is
also what an AB or MOS listening test needs, so this page is the listening-test
material rather than only a demo.

## Why a static page

Nothing here runs at request time: the audio, the token sequences and the
provenance of every clip are files, generated by `scripts/render_demo.py` and
committed. The cost is that the sentence set is fixed. That is a requirement of
a listening test rather than a limitation of it — a pre-registered set is the
point — and it means the page keeps working after the GPU access that trained
these models has gone.

Arbitrary text needs a Python runtime, because the front end here is the front
end that trained the weights, down to the normaliser and the schwa
configuration. A reimplementation in JavaScript could differ in exactly the
place this study makes its claim.

## How the sentences were chosen

From the held-out dev split, which no run trained on, filtered to a listening
length, then banded by how much medial schwa deletion each sentence contains
and taken from the richest band down, sorted by utterance id so the selection
is deterministic given the split. The exact rule, the band sizes and the
per-sentence counts are in `data.json`.

Word-final deletion is reported but not selected on: it is near-categorical in
Hindi and both arms learn it. Medial deletion is the conditioned case where
they should come apart. Sentences with no medial deletion are kept as a
control.

## What is on the page, and what is not

The arms are grouped by what they share: the comparison itself, the same arm at
other seeds, and the same arm trained on less data. The seed groups are there
because training noise is the scale any claimed difference has to beat.

The FastSpeech 2 arms are not intelligible in this build, and the page says so
where they appear. The cause is measured and is in the training objective, not
in the ablation and not in the vocoder: the loss sums a mel term against pitch
and energy terms left in physical units, so the mel decoder was optimised
against well under one per cent of the gradient. Those rows are a record of
what was run. They are not a result about phonemic against graphemic input.

## Regenerating

    python scripts/build_demo_set.py --lang hindi --count 12
    python scripts/render_demo.py   --lang hindi \
        --vocoder exports/r06_step18000

Without `--vocoder` the mel-only arms write no audio and publish as silent.
Each language has its own vocoder, so a Marathi render passes the Marathi
bundle instead; the rate and the language are checked before anything renders.
